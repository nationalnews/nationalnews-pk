The International Monetary Fund has forecast a 1.3% contraction in the combined Middle East, North Africa and Pakistan region in 2026, according to a Reuters report published on 22 October. The report attributes the outlook to the continuing war involving Iran and the resulting disruption to energy production, shipping, tourism and trade.

The regional figure should not be read as a forecast that Pakistan’s economy alone will shrink by 1.3%. It covers a group of economies, and their exposure to the conflict varies. The IMF has also warned that a prolonged conflict could worsen the outlook, while a recovery is anticipated in 2027.

Why this matters for Pakistan

For Pakistan, disruption to global energy supplies and shipping can raise import costs and add pressure to prices, businesses and the external account. The scale of any impact depends on how long disruptions last, international energy prices, remittance flows and policy responses. This is economic context, not a prediction of a specific change in local prices.

What to watch next

  • Further IMF updates and country-specific forecasts for Pakistan.
  • International oil and freight costs, which influence import bills.
  • Official data on inflation, trade and foreign-exchange reserves.
  • Whether diplomatic efforts reduce disruption to regional trade and shipping.
